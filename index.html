Stuti @stuti • Community & Privacy
4h

Identity is not the same thing as identity proof.

Most social platforms ask you to build a recognizable identity: your name, your face, your followers, your history, your activity. Over time, that identity becomes the product.

Utopia takes a different approach. Here, you can participate without having to turn your real-world identity into your social identity. A pseudonym can be enough.

Be yourself without fear. You should be able to build a reputation through what you contribute — not through how much of your personal life you reveal.

Utopia is pseudonymous by design. People shouldn't have to attach their real-world identity to every opinion, interest, or conversation. The goal is to give people room to express themselves honestly while still maintaining accountability through community rules and moderation.

That doesn't mean anonymity without consequences. Utopia is designed around pseudonymity with accountability:

— Communities can have moderators
— Rules can be enforced
— Abuse can be reported
— Sanctions can be applied

Privacy isn't a feature we add later or a setting buried in a menu. It affects how the system is designed from the beginning. Member lists can remain hidden. Participation is scoped to communities. Sessions are protected.

Utopia is centered around communities and conversations rather than follower counts, popularity contests, and personal branding.

We want to keep that boundary. You shouldn't have to reveal your life to have a voice.

Adi @adi • Systems
2h

Utopia is a social media platform that doesn't beg for your attention.

It isn't designed to keep you scrolling for hours, maximize engagement, or constantly sell you something.

Utopia is built around a simple idea: Social media should serve people, not exploit them.

The platform should help people find communities they genuinely care about, express themselves freely, discover things that are actually relevant to them, and leave when they're done.

What makes Utopia different:

No attention manipulation. We don't want dark patterns, addictive interaction loops, endless notifications, or interfaces designed purely to keep people online.

An algorithm that works for you. Recommendations should prioritize relevance and usefulness over engagement at any cost. We want to help users find things they actually care about — not show whatever is most likely to keep them scrolling.

How Utopia should feel:

— Calm, not chaotic
— Human, not corporate
— Simple, not empty
— Expressive, not attention-seeking
— Private, not secretive
— Community-driven, not popularity-driven
— Intentional, not addictive

The frictionless exit: A user should be able to open Utopia, find something meaningful, participate in a conversation, and close the app without feeling like the product is trying to pull them back in.

Don't design Utopia to look like an existing social network with a different color palette. The interaction model itself should reflect the philosophy.

Neo @neo • Design Director
55m

Utopia is built around a simple idea: your digital spaces should belong to you.

Not to an algorithm. Not to an engagement dashboard. Not to a company deciding what you should see, how you should behave, or who you should become.

We imagine communities that feel more like spaces than feeds. Places where people can gather around shared interests, create their own culture, and decide what belongs there.

Imagine opening Utopia and finding a space that feels unmistakably yours. The way you arrange things. The way it moves. The tools you choose. Like configuring a desktop environment — except the environment is social.

Some people might want something minimal. Some might want something chaotic. Some might build something completely unfamiliar. That freedom is the point.

The core principle: Every UI decision should answer one question: Does this make Utopia more useful to the person using it, or does it merely make them stay longer?

If something increases engagement but makes the experience worse for the user, we choose the user.

We're building the foundation first: pseudonymous identity, communities, moderation, accountability, privacy, and user control. Then we'll see where the community takes it.
